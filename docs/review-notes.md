# Review notes: points to check first

All content is an unreviewed draft written with Claude. While drafting, the authors flagged the
points below as uncertain or as varying between sources. A reviewer should start with these.

## Terminology
- **Swedish muscle names** are mostly in Wikipedia style ("korta tumutåtförarmuskeln", "tummens
  motställarmuskel", "maskmusklerna", "armbågsböjaren" for m. brachialis, "korpnäbbsarmmuskeln",
  "armbågsmuskeln", "brachioradialismuskeln", "långa tumutåtföraren"). Check which forms KI teaching uses;
  KI often uses the Latin name directly.
- **Swedish carpal and landmark names:** "trekantiga benet", "huvudbenet", "hakbenet", "griffelutskott",
  "strålbensknölen", "flexor-/extensorretinaklet".
- **Swedish plexus terms:** "primärsträng" / "sekundärsträng" for truncus / fasciculus.
- **Hungarian names:** "felkarizom" (m. brachialis), "hollócsőr-karizom" (m. coracobrachialis),
  "könyöknyújtvány" (olecranon), "felkar-orsócsonti izom" (m. brachioradialis), "hanyintóizom"
  (m. supinator), "bökőnyúlvány" (processus styloideus), "orsócsont-érdesség", "hüvelykujj-szembefordító"
  vs "-szembesítő izom", "háromszögletű csont", "tenyéri bőnye", "hajlítóín-/feszítőín-rögzítő szalag".
- **Mnemonics** that may not work in Hungarian or Swedish: "LOAF" (kz13), "TAN" (ko7), and
  "Rika Tanter Dricker Fint Rödvin" (q16, Swedish version).

## Anatomy facts that vary by source
- **Spinal segments:** m. coracobrachialis C5–C7; m. brachialis C5–C6, often with an extra n. radialis
  branch (C7); m. triceps brachii C6–C8; m. subscapularis C5–C7 (many sources give C5–C6); FDS C7–T1;
  FPL and m. pronator quadratus C8–T1; ECRB C7–C8; m. supinator C5–C6.
- **ECRB:** the draft gives the deep branch of n. radialis; some sources give the superficial branch.
- **Thumb CMC joint innervation:** the draft lists articular branches of the median, superficial radial and
  ulnar nerves. Sources differ.
- **m. anconeus:** the draft says it slightly abducts the ulna in pronation, with blood supply from
  a. interossea recurrens / a. collateralis media.
- **m. palmaris longus:** absent in about 10–15% (published ranges are wider).

## Normal values and clinical statements
- **ROM norms:**
  - elbow flexion 150°
  - pronation and supination 80° each
  - wrist flexion 80°, extension 70°, radial deviation 20°, ulnar deviation 30°
  - MCP flexion 90°, extension 45°, abduction 20° (no AAOS value for abduction)
  - PIP 100°, DIP 90°
  - CMC I flexion 15°, extension 20°, abduction 70°
- **Functional ranges:** elbow 30–130°, pronation and supination about 50° each (Morrey); wrist about
  40° flexion and 40° extension (Ryu).
- **Carrying angle:** about 10–15°, usually larger in women.
- **Wrist capsular pattern:** flexion and extension about equally limited (Cyriax).
- **Safe splint position:** MCP 70–90°, wrist about 20–30° extension. This is a common convention,
  not a single cited source.
- **Humeral-shaft radial nerve injury with triceps spared (ko2):** the typical pattern, not a rule.
- **ko3:** "screws are right-hand threaded because supination is stronger" is a teaching anecdote.
- **Skier's thumb (UCL):** described without a degree cut-off.
- **kz16:** the order of the deep ulnar branch is simplified.
- **Test names to check:** Cozen, moving valgus stress, Hook test, elbow flexion test, Schaeffer test
  (palmaris longus), Finkelstein, Eichhoff, Froment, grind test.

## Lower limb (modules csipo, comb-terd, labszar, labfej)
- **Hungarian names:**
  - muscles: "belső/külső csípőlyukizom", "combpólyafeszítő izom", "karcsú izom", "külső/belső/középső vaskos izom", "térdalji izom", "félig inas / félig hártyás izom", "talpizom" (plantaris), "gázlóizom" (soleus)
  - landmarks: "csípővápa", "lúdláb", "csípő-sípcsonti szalagköteg", "sípcsont-érdesség", "kakaslépés" (steppage gait)
- **Swedish names:**
  - muscles: "fascia lata-spännaren", "smala lårmuskeln", "tarmbensmuskeln", "inre/yttre obturatormuskeln", "laterala/mediala/mellersta vida lårmuskeln", "knävecksmuskeln", "halvsenmuskeln", "halvhinnmuskeln", "plantarismuskeln", "flundremuskeln", "tredje vadbensmuskeln", "stortåns inåtförarmuskel", "fyrkantiga fotsulemuskeln"
  - other terms: "höftledspanna", "patellarsenan", "kuboidbenet (tärningsbenet)", "tennisvad"
- **Spinal segments that vary by source:**
  - hip: piriformis S1–S2 (or L5–S2), psoas major L1–L3 (or L1–L4), adductor magnus (tibial part L4, obturator part L2–L4), gemellus inferior L4–S1
  - thigh and knee: sartorius L2–L3, quadriceps L2–L4, hamstrings L5–S2, popliteus L4–S1
  - leg and foot: FHL, FDL and plantaris S1–S2, tibialis posterior L4–L5 (or L5–S1), plantar nerves S1–S2 (Kendall: medial plantar L4–S1)
- **ROM norms (AAOS-style):**
  - hip: flexion 120, extension 20, abduction 45, adduction 30, rotation 45/45
  - knee: flexion 135 (some sources 140–150)
  - ankle: dorsiflexion 20, plantarflexion 50
  - subtalar: inversion 35, eversion 15 (calcaneal-angle methods give smaller values)
  - 1st MTP: extension 70 (70–90), flexion 45; about 60° extension needed in gait
- **Clinical figures and claims:**
  - Q-angle 10–15°; VMO fibre angle 50–55°; navicular drop >10 mm
  - MTSS ≥5 cm vs stress fracture <5 cm; Achilles rupture 2–6 cm above the insertion
  - sciatic nerve through piriformis in about 10–15%; plantaris absent in 7–10%
  - hip capsular pattern internal rotation > flexion > abduction (Cyriax)
  - hip OA criteria NICE-style (age ≥45, morning stiffness ≤30 min)
  - "unhappy triad" in its classic form (with a note on the lateral meniscus)
  - midtarsal locking in the classic Elftman model (newer work disputes it)
  - Baxter nerve course; Morton's neuroma in the 3rd interspace
  - anterior drawer most reliable 4–5 days after injury
- **Open terminology choices:**
  - category of tractus iliotibialis, tendo calcaneus and the retinacula ("muscles")
  - articulatio tarsometatarsalis (TA uses the plural)
  - interossei pedis kept as one record
- **No 3D mesh** for extensor digitorum brevis, quadratus plantae or the interossei of the foot (not in BodyParts3D).
